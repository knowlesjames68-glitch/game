# The Pond's Pulse

An interactive ecosystem-management game where players maintain the long-term health and biodiversity of a living pond.

## Project Vision

**The Pond's Pulse** is designed around a core idea: the gameplay loop should mirror real biological and environmental cycles.

Players take the role of an ecosystem caretaker and repeatedly make choices that influence:

- the **water cycle** (rainfall, evaporation, water quality)
- the **food chain** (algae → insects → fish → frogs)
- **seasonal cycles** (temperature shifts, breeding windows, resource fluctuations)

The game demonstrates that repetition in nature is not just pattern—it is stability. When loops are disrupted, ecosystems become fragile.

## Core Gameplay Loop

1. **Observe** pond conditions and species populations.
2. **Intervene** by introducing species, planting vegetation, or reducing pollution.
3. **Simulate** cycle outcomes across days/seasons.
4. **Respond** to new risks (drought, disease, invasive species).
5. **Evaluate** ecosystem health and biodiversity score.
6. Repeat with increasing complexity.

This repeated loop is both the player's strategy cycle and the game’s educational model of natural cycles.

## Systems to Manage

### Species Balance
- Algae
- Insects
- Fish
- Frogs
- Optional unlocks: birds, snails, amphibian variants, predator fish

### Environmental Health
- Water quality
- Temperature
- Nutrient levels
- Vegetation cover
- Pollution index

### Dynamic Events
- Droughts
- Disease outbreaks
- Invasive species
- Sudden nutrient bloom/algae overgrowth

### Seasonal Variation
Each season shifts:
- breeding patterns
- food availability
- oxygen and nutrient dynamics
- event probabilities

## Progression and Success Metrics

Player progression is measured through **long-term ecosystem stability** rather than short-term resource gain.

### Primary Score
- Ecosystem Health Index (aggregate system stability)
- Biodiversity Score (species richness and sustained population balance)

### Unlock Paths
- New species to manage
- New pond biomes / connected wetlands
- Advanced tools for restoration and monitoring

## Learning Goals

Players should come away understanding that:

- ecosystems rely on interconnected cycles
- repeated actions can stabilize or destabilize the system
- sustainability is about long-term balance, not maximum growth

## Why This Fits the Loop & Repetition Theme

This project directly applies game-loop theory to science content:

- **Micro loops**: frequent player decisions (adjust, monitor, react)
- **Meso loops**: seasonal adaptation cycles
- **Macro loops**: long-term ecosystem stewardship and recovery

By aligning gameplay repetition with environmental repetition, the project turns abstract cycle concepts into interactive cause-and-effect learning.
